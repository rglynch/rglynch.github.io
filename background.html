Background

I build AI-assisted systems and the validation around them

Career Path

My PhD research was in frame theory and compressive sensing, both squarely in applied math. Frames are overcomplete systems of vectors that generalize orthonormal bases, useful when stability and redundancy matter. Compressive sensing is about recovering sparse signals from far fewer measurements than classical sampling theory requires. Both connect to signal processing, imaging, and numerical linear algebra.

I finished my PhD at the University of Missouri-Columbia in 2016 under Peter Casazza, then spent five years at Texas A&M as a Visiting Assistant Professor and then Instructional Assistant Professor. That was the last stretch of about nine years of university teaching between 2010 and 2021, starting as a graduate assistant at SIUE and Missouri. I taught a lot of calculus and linear algebra and got reasonably good at explaining hard things to people seeing them for the first time.

From 2021 to 2026 I worked in educational technology at Edfinity (Looking Glass Ventures), an online assessment platform for university math courses, covering both graded coursework and secure high-stakes testing. I built and maintained the technical content systems, worked with university partners on course setup and adoption, and authored thousands of WeBWorK problems in PG/Perl, for courses from college algebra through linear algebra.

What I kept gravitating toward was the machinery around the work rather than the work itself: getting dependable output from early AI models, simulating a generator to find where its behavior held, rebuilding numerical routines until they matched independent implementations, and being the person a customer called when something was wrong in production. That is what I look for now, and I would rather be close to where the thing gets used than several steps upstream of it.

Interests

I follow what's happening in compressed sensing and sparse methods, particularly where they connect to modern machine learning. One example: in MRI reconstruction, sparsity-based approaches are increasingly being replaced or supplemented by generative priors. That kind of shift, where classical frameworks get extended or retired, is worth paying attention to.

I also spend time building tools and automating things, and explaining technical material to non-specialists. Both showed up regularly in my work.
